## SDLC (Mnemonic: RADDTURM)

- Design
  - Requirements Gathering
  - Analyzing Requirements
  - Design
- Development
  - Develop
  - Test
  - User Acceptance Testing (UAT)
- Delivery
  - Release
  - Maintenance

## Methodologies & Frameworks

- **Framework**: a specific implementation of a methodology's concepts.
- **Methodology**: category of concepts and practices to carry out the SDLC.
  - **Big Bang**: jump straight into project development.
  - **Waterfall**: the steps of SDLC are completed in order.
    - Myth: you can't go back to a previous step.
    - Like a highway: if you miss your exit, it wastes time but you can always loop back around.
  - **Agile**: flexible, iterative approach to software development.
    - Collaboration between developers, stakeholders, and customers.
    - Adaptability: responding to changing requirements.
    - Incremental delivery: smaller working software delivered early and often.

## Agile

- Not a single method, but a mindset.
- Agile Manifesto:
  - Individuals & interactions over processes and tools.
  - Working software over comprehensive documentation.
  - Customer collaboration over contract negotiation.
  - Responding to change over following a plan.
- Flavors:
  - **Scrum**: work is divided into sprints (typically 2-4 weeks), with roles (scrum master, product owner, development team).
  - **Kanban**: visualizes work on a board and focuses on the flow of work.
  - **XP**: emphasizes engineering best practices (pair programming, TDD).
  - **Lean**: adapts manufacturing principles to software.
  - **SAFe** (Scaled Agile Framework): needed for large organizations that need to deploy agile at scale.

## Agile Ceremonies

- **Sprints**: fixed time box of incremental & shippable work.
- **Sprint planning**: meeting at the start of a sprint that pulls work from backlog, schedules the work, & assigns story points.
- **Daily standup**: short ~15 daily (or biweekly/weekly) sync where team members share:
  - What they did yesterday.
  - What they'll do today.
  - Any blockers.
- **Sprint review**: end-of-sprint meeting to show completed work to stakeholders & gather feedback.
- **Sprint retrospective**: team-only meeting to reflect on the sprint & identify improvements for the next one.
- **Backlog refinement (grooming)**: ongoing sessions to clarify, estimate, & prioritize backlog items, so they're ready for future sprints.
- **Release planning**: higher-level planning to determine when features or products will be delivered across multiple sprints.

## Agile Planning

- **User Story**: an individual feature of/requirement for a project in Agile.
- **User Epic**: a group of related features that are broken down into multiple stories.
- **Story Points**: a level of difficulty assigned to a user story, through use of a sequence of numbers that increase with difficulty.
- **Velocity**: sum of story points of all stories completed during a sprint; allows a more accurate prediction of how many stories can be completed in future sprints.
