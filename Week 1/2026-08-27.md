## AI vs. ML

- **AI**: broad field of CS focused on building systems that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence:
  - Problem solving
  - Pattern recognition
  - Language understanding
  - Decision making
  - Perception (vision, speech)
- AI is an umbrella term that includes rule-based systems, machine learning, robotics, NLP.
- **ML**: Subset of AI where systems learn patterns from data instead of being explicitly programmed w/ rules.
  - Instead of `if X then Y`, it's more like `data → learning → prediction`.
  - Predict or classify.

## Generative AI

- **Engineering**: clear instructions, context, constraints, desired format, examples; often faster and more cost-effective than retraining or fine-tuning a model.
- **Zero-shot prompting**: When you ask a model to perform a task without providing examples.
  - Fast & flexible.
  - Minimal setup.
  - Less reliable for complex or specialized tasks.
- **Constraints**: instructions added to a prompt to guide how the model responds.
- **Fine-tuning**: retraining a pre-trained model on additional domain-specific data to adjust its behavior permanently.
- **Hallucinations**: presents false data confidently:
  - Gaps in training data.
  - No built-in truth-checking mechanism.
  - Ambiguous or under-specified prompts.
  - LLMs predict likely next tokens, not verified facts.
- **Hallucination mitigation strategies**:
  - Add constraints (“Only use provided data”).
  - Use Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).
  - Require citations.
  - Human review.
- **RAG**: combines a language model with external data retrieval.
- Prompt types: instruction/completion/dialogue/chain-of-thought.
