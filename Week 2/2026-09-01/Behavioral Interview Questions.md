  <summary>
    <b>2. Tell me about a time you went above and beyond the expectation</b>
  </summary>

  <h3>Situation</h3>
  <p>
    During the beginning stages of my Application Security internship at Paycom,
    I had complete a Capture The Flag challenge, which is also called a CTF for
    short. A CTF is an intentionally vulnerably built website that contains
    hidden flags that could be found by using some form of exploitation of a
    security vulnerability. The object of any CTF is to find as many flags as
    possible within a specified period of time to practice your hacking skills.
  </p>

  <h3>Task</h3>
  <p>
    During the final stages of my internship, I needed to choose a project to
    work on before wrapping up. The project I chose required me to work in a
    team with two other interns to patch up all of the security vulnerabilities
    of the CTF we completed in the first stage of our internship in order to
    sharpen our code analysis and vulnerability mitigation skills.
  </p>

  <h3>Action</h3>
  <p>
    My team and I worked to effectively divide and conquer all of the tasks and
    completed our project far before our deadline. Since we had a lot of extra
    time, we decided to expand the scope of the original project and add new
    hidden flags with unique exploitation strategies for the future cohort of
    interns to find.
  </p>
  <p>
    On top of that, I also asked for permission to redesign the entire website
    and give it a completely new and modern look. I have a strong passion for
    front end web development and wanted to show off my UI skills here too,
    even though my internship revolved around security.
  </p>

  <h3>Result</h3>
  <p>
    After submitting my pull request and demoing the new CTF with a UI
    overhaul, I got lots of positive feedback from leadership and got the green
    light to officially merge my changes into the main branch. My changes are
    now a permanent part of Paycom's CTF website to this day and every future
    cohort of interns will see what I have contributed.
  </p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>4. Tell me about a time you worked with someone difficult</b></summary>

  <h3>Situation</h3>
  <p>
    As a tutor, I'm constantly working with a wide variety of students with
    many different learning styles. Often times, students can be very difficult
    to work with, and I need to adapt to each and every student to meet them
    where they're at in order to carry out a productive tutoring session.
  </p>

  <h3>Task</h3>
  <p>
    Quite frequently, I would have to deal with students who just want answers and
    don't care too much about their work or students who are far behind and don't
    have the necessary prerequisite knowledge.
  </p>

  <h3>Action</h3>
  <p>
    I always start every session with socratic questioning to get a baseline on
    the student's current level of understanding on whatever topic they need help
    with. Some students come in and simply ask for a direct answer. As a tutor,
    my job is to teach, not to give answers. So I need redirect them to give the
    problem a try, take a look at their notes, or look at previous similar
    problems they have already done. Then I can figure out their knowledge gaps
    and begin teaching at a level that makes sense to them specifically.
  </p>

  <h3>Result</h3>
  <p>
    At the end of the day, when I'm working with difficult students, I always try
    to pivot their attitude to be more positive and to enjoy learning. I want to
    explain the what, the how, and the why. I like giving students the light bulb
    moment, and I want them to believe that they could've discovered the solution
    on their own. I have a passion for sharing my knowledge in the world of math
    and computer science in a way that makes it understandable and and gets
    students interested in learning more. I think this makes every one of my
    tutoring sessions more productive for both me and the student.
  </p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>8. Tell me a time when you had to be proactive</b></summary>

  <h3>Situation</h3>
  <p>
    During my time as a software developer at Paycom, my team spent a lot of
    time manually code reviewing other pull requests from other teams. This was
    because my team dealt with user roles, and you can imagine that every other
    team would need to deal with checking roles and their authorizations or deal
    with adding and deleting roles. Unfortunately, the architecture of our role
    system was not initially built to scale easily, and we were still maintaining
    the old infrastructure since doing a rewrite wouldn't be feasible. The process
    to create a new role and add permissions was very lengthy and needed to be
    done manually. This means incorrectly adding, editing, or deleting roles
    happened quite frequently.
  </p>
  <p>
    Our repository required approvals from other teams whenever a file owned by
    that team was modified. For my team specifically, we were regularly receiving
    5-10 code review requests per day due to the nature of the ownership of our 
    code. Performing manual code reviews slowed down our own development quite a
    bit, and we found it to be annoying.
  </p>

  <h3>Task</h3>
  <p>
    My team leader assigned me the task of creating a new documentation page to
    concisely list out the steps such that they are easy to follow and unambiguous.
  </p>

  <h3>Action</h3>
  <p>
    I did exactly that and also came up with a new "self approval" system. This
    new system allowed other teams to tag us in their pull requests and basically
    say "I confirm that I read the documentation entirely and followed all the
    steps exactly". This shifted the responsibility to other teams and allowed 
    us to provide instant approvals without needing to waste time manually reading
    the code.
  </p>

  <h3>Result</h3>
  <p>
    This new system that we ended up rolling out helped improve the efficiency of
    other teams and also significantly cut down the wasted amount of time and 
    effort we had to spend manually reviewing pull requests from other teams. This
    bought us a lot more time for us to focus on our own work.
  </p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>9. Tell me about a time you were under a lot of pressure</b></summary>

  <h3>Situation</h3>
  <p>
    During my Full Stack Engineering internship at Paper.co, I spent a lot of time
    setting up my local development environment on my first day.
  </p>

  <h3>Task</h3>
  <p>
    My very first ticket was to simply update the copy for a modal. I thought this
    would be a super easy 3 minute task that I could finish before my lunch break,
    but I was very wrong.
  </p>

  <h3>Action</h3>
  <p>
    It took a bit of time to find the proper component since I wasn't aware of my
    IDE's global text search at that time. Once I found the component, I couldn't
    even find the text I needed to replace. This was because the string text was in
    a completely different translations file. At that time, I wasn't aware of 
    internationalizations and translation keys. I did a bit more research and found
    the correct translation key for the modal. However, saving the file didn't
    automatically update the modal's text, even after killing and restarting the
    entire local development server. By this time, my lunch break had already
    started, but I decided to work through it in order to get my ticket completed.
    After some time, I found out that there was a script to compile translation keys
    and was finally able to submit my first pull request. 
  </p>

  <h3>Result</h3>
  <p>
    Working through my lunch break definitely saved me a bit of embarrassment
    since this was supposed to be a very easy introductory ticket. Being under
    a lot of pressure, it encouraged me to learn more and understand how
    internationalization standards are implemented in modern web applications.
    This helped me look a bit more competent in front of my manager, and I was
    able to work on a more meaningful ticket immediately after.
  </p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary><b>10. Tell me about a time you had a conflict/disagreement with a coworker</b></summary>

  <h3>Situation</h3>
  <p>
    During my time as a Software Developer at Paycom, I experienced a company
    restructuring and my team lost some important people, including our project
    manager. My team leader stated that all individual contributors (developers)
    now owned the product decisions that the previous project manager would've
    been in charge of. I picked up a ticket to implement a simple-sounding
    feature, but after more careful consideration, I realized it required more
    clarity. This was been something that I would typically discuss with our
    previous project manager.
  </p>

  <h3>Task</h3>
  <p>
    The actual issue is a bit too complex to explain concisely, but it required
    something to happen whenever a feature flag got toggled to migrate to a ne
     system or migrate back to the old system. There were multiple behaviors
     that could possibly happen because there was not a one-to-one mapping
     between the old and new system.
  </p>

  <h3>Action</h3>
  <p>
    Since this was my ticket and I was in charge of making a decision, I wanted
    to implement it in a way that was most intuitive to me. I consulted a senior
    developer on my team about my implementation strategy and got an approval as
    well as an architecture suggestion. After pushing my code as well as detailed 
    testing notes, I received feedback from a newer developer asking why I had
    "confusing and redundant" testing steps. He failed to realize there were
    multiple ways to implement the mappings for my feature and multiple test
    scenarios were needed to cover all cases. Because of this, we discussed our
    different approaches but had our own opinions on what was and wasn't "more
    intuitive." I pulled in my team leader into the discussion and we came to
    the conclusion that either way was valid, as long as it worked.</p>

  <h3>Result</h3>
  <p>
    Since my code was already completed and making additional changes wasn't
    unnecessary, I got the green light to merge my feature.
  </p>
</details>
